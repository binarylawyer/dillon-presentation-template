## 1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

### 1.1 What this deal is

The **Troika District** is a Phase 1 redevelopment of **ten historic commercial buildings on West Main Street, Dillon, South Carolina** — four already owned by JD McLeod — into a destination culinary, retail, hospitality, and wellness corridor anchored to the City's adjacent Marketplace Commons. It is offered as a **tokenized Qualified Opportunity Fund** (Troika Main Street QOF, LLC) raising **$4,500,000–$6,000,000 in LP equity** under **Regulation D Rule 506(c)**, with the Manager authorized to increase the raise to a maximum of **$7,500,000**.

The deal captures an unusually deep, codified incentive stack — OZ 2.0 (rural), federal and SC historic tax credits, and confirmed New Markets Tax Credit eligibility at the Severe Distress tier — against distressed acquisition pricing ($50K–$200K per building) and stabilized per-property values of $600K–$2.35M. A licensed-GC developer who self-performs construction compresses cost further.

### 1.2 The investment thesis (four pillars)

1. **Codified incentive convergence.** OZ 2.0 (permanent under the OBBBA, signed July 4, 2025), 20% federal + ~15% blended SC historic tax credits, and 39% NMTC — stackable on the same properties — plus grants. These are statutory, not speculative.
2. **Entry basis far below replacement.** Downtown Dillon commercial buildings trade at $50K–$200K each; stabilized values run $600K–$2.35M — a 4–10x value-creation spread.
3. **Self-performed construction.** JD is a licensed SC General Contractor who owns 4 of the 10 properties and self-performs the work through Troika Group of the Carolinas, saving an estimated $750K–$2M versus a third-party GC.
4. **First-mover position.** No comparable boutique culinary/hospitality district exists in downtown Dillon; the City-funded Marketplace Commons sits immediately behind the block as a built-in traffic generator.

## 2. The Syndicate — Leadership, Economics & Governance

### 2.1 What the syndicate is

The deal is led by **the syndicate** — a separate, newly formed joint-venture company that JD McLeod and Christopher Moye **co-founded** to manage and support this deal, combining their respective skill sets. It functions like many joint ventures: a combination of complementary capabilities under one roof, distinct from either founder's existing businesses.

The syndicate is **not** Troika Group of the Carolinas (JD's general-contracting company) and **not** Christopher's software company. JD and Christopher are **co-founders of the syndicate, not of Troika.** The syndicate operates through the GP entity, **Troika Capital GP, LLC** (name retained by agreement), which is the **managing member of the fund** and holds all GP economics.

### 2.2 Leadership & roles

| Role | Person | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **CEO** | **JD McLeod** | Overall leadership; lead developer; GC of record (through Troika Group); on-the-ground operations, construction, property management, municipal and contractor relationships |
| **COO** | **Christopher Moye** | Deal architecture; capital raise & investor relations; securities/tokenization framework; SEC compliance; deal technology (software licensed in at market rate) |

Both are **managing members of Troika Capital GP, LLC**.

> **Title-vs-function note (for the PPM bios).** JD also carries the operational/GC execution on the ground, which is the function a "COO" title usually signals. The titles here are a deliberate positioning choice by the founders; the bios should let the *role descriptions* carry the substance so the titles and the functional reality read consistently to investors and counsel.

### 2.3 Economics — the governing 60/40 split

The syndicate's economics are split **60% JD / 40% Christopher** of all GP economics (the 30% promote + all GP fees). **Every published document must reflect this 60/40 number.**

Each founder also has compensation flowing **outside** the equity split, through arms-length affiliate arrangements:
- **JD:** construction work billed through **Troika Group of the Carolinas** (licensed GC) at arms-length/market rates.
- **Christopher:** **software licensed in at market rate** (exact price TBD) through his software company.

So total economics to each founder = their share of GP (60/40) **plus** their respective affiliate compensation. Both affiliate arrangements are **disclosed related-party transactions** (see §3 and §10).

### 2.4 Governance items to paper in the GP operating agreement

- **Decision authority:** CEO (JD) — operations, construction, property management, tenant relations. COO (Christopher) — capital, investor relations, reporting, compliance, legal/tokenization. Major decisions (acquisitions above a threshold, dispositions, refinancing, new debt) require mutual agreement.
- **Deadlock resolution:** mediation first, then buyout provisions with a defined valuation methodology.
- **Capital-call obligations** between the founders, if additional GP capital is required.
- **Exit / buyout** pricing and non-compete on founder departure.
- **Key-person provisions:** the QOF Op. Agr. already designates JD as Key Person with a 90-day successor window; mirror appropriate key-person protection for both founders, and consider life insurance.

## 7. Investor Identification & 506(c) Compliance Framework

### 7.1 506(c) — what changes vs. 506(b)

This deal is **Rule 506(c)**, which **permits general solicitation** but requires that **all investors be accredited** and that the issuer take **reasonable steps to verify** accredited status (not mere self-certification). Verification runs through the Metallicus WebAuth KYC/AML flow plus an accreditation-verification method (income, net-worth, or third-party letter from a CPA/attorney/broker-dealer).

Practical implications:
- **No non-accredited investors** — the leftover "35 non-accredited" clause in the Op. Agr. must be struck (§3, §10).
- General solicitation is allowed, but **verification is mandatory and must be documented** for every investor.
- Maintain an audit trail of verification evidence per investor.

### 7.2 Investor pipeline (four profiles)

| Profile | Why this deal fits | Expected check |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Tech founders/execs with recent liquidity** (Christopher's network) | OZ gain exclusion solves a live capital-gains problem; analytical buyers fluent in "compressed basis" | $200K–$500K |
| **B — Entertainment industry** (Christopher's network) | Lumpy income, perpetual tax planning; HTC pass-through + OZ shelter | $150K–$300K |
| **C — Real estate professionals / syndication investors** (Christopher's network) | Understand the vehicle natively; the 55–70% combined credit stack on rural QROF-enhanced buildings is the novelty | $150K–$250K |
| **D — JD's local Dillon County network** | Live in the market; local pride + tax benefits | $50K–$150K (must still be accredited under 506(c)) |

### 7.3 Funnel math

At a $4.5M–$6.0M raise with a $50K minimum, the fund can accommodate a wide investor count. At higher average checks ($150K–$300K from Profiles A–C), the raise can close with a few dozen committed investors. Anchor commitments from 2–3 tech investors with significant recent gains create momentum.
